# Controller support: changelog lines for when the controller branch is released

(2026-11-23: moved into changelog.md under ## 0.14.2, with the controller brought onto main. Kept for the record.)

(Not in any release yet. Taken out of changelog.md on 2026-11-21: 0.12.0 was released without it.)

- Controller: with the game's gamepad interface on (WoW Forever's Gamepad UI), the quest window is made for the controller, after the game's own quest log beside the map, and switches with the interface as it changes (in a fight, once it is over). It joins the game's navigation (LT/RT go between it and the map) and the game's prompt bar under it names every button that works. The window is one pane: the list, and A on a quest shows its page in the list's place; B goes back one step (out of the search, from the page to that quest in the list, out of the settings or the chronicle) before it closes the window and the map. Each quest is one stop for the cursor, which starts on the quest that is open. X tracks or untracks the quest, held puts it in focus or lets go; on Available X shows the quest's giver on the map, on Dungeons the entrance. Y opens the quest's menu (Share, Abandon...); on its page, the page's buttons. LB/RB change tabs, View goes to the search box, the right stick scrolls the page, A on the page's title links the quest in chat. The party on a quest's page is a row a member, with where they are with it; A shares the quest with a member who can take it, Y whispers. With a full log, Available opens with a banner that takes you to the quests worth dropping; abandoning always asks first (the game's own question).
- Controller, the settings and the chronicle: their sections down the side show as the cursor comes onto them, LB/RB go to the section before or after, A goes into the one shown and B back to its row, then out. Reset to defaults is under Y; in the journal, B closes a quest's story before anything else.
- Controller, the tracker: the game's quests shortcut (the shortcuts' modifier held, d-pad up) gives OneMoreQuest's tracker the cursor when it stands in for the game's. Each quest is one stop: A focuses it (or lets go), X opens it in the quest log, Y has its menu, where its item can be put on the gamepad's action bar; B goes back to the game.
- Controller, the map: the map's own cursor points at OneMoreQuest's marks as at the game's pins, with their tooltip and what A does; A opens the quest's page in the window. Quest areas and spawns under the cursor show their tooltip too.
